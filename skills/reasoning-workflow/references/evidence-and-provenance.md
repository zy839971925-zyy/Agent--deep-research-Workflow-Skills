# Evidence and provenance

**Trigger:** External, disputed, multi-source, weak-signal, quantitative, private, or consequential evidence matters.

**Reads:** material claims/questions, observations, source material, evidence gaps.

**Updates:** evidence records, source-position assessment, provenance clusters, support relations, confidence/limitations.

**May invalidate:** claims, inferences, decisions, recommendations, artifacts based on misapplied or copied evidence.

**Exit:** material claims are supported, contested, inaccessible, or bounded at the needed level.

**Related:** [retrieval and observation](retrieval-and-observation.md), [inquiry and research](inquiry-and-research.md), [traceability and integrity](traceability-and-integrity.md).

**Return:** [root workflow](../SKILL.md) → Evidence judgment.

Use when external claims, multiple sources, weak signals, disputes, or high-consequence conclusions need traceable support.

## Separate observation, assertion, and inference

Do not let a source citation blur distinct propositions. Split compound claims when support differs. Useful records are:

- **Claim:** precise proposition, scope/time, importance, evidence links, current state.
- **Evidence:** source/version, locator, inspected material or faithful observation, access extent, origin, limitations.
- **Support link:** supports / contradicts / mixed / context only, with applicability.
- **Inference:** conclusion, premises, bridge, assumptions, counterevidence, and reversal conditions.

“Verified” should mean that a source or operation was actually inspected/performed, not that objective truth has been proven.

## Evaluate evidence by fit to the claim

Authority is one signal, not a universal ranking. Consider:

- **position to know / directness:** could this source actually observe the fact?
- **provenance independence:** does it originate independently or copy another source?
- **method fitness:** was the observation or study capable of answering the proposition?
- **specificity/applicability:** same entity, version, population, jurisdiction, metric, and context?
- **temporal fit:** right period and governing version?
- **traceability:** can the supporting material be inspected and located?
- **incentives and conflicts:** what reasons exist to frame, omit, exaggerate, or under-report?
- **cross-evidence consistency:** do sources with different error mechanisms converge?

An official statement is excellent evidence of what an institution officially said; it is not automatically independent proof of every underlying assertion.

## Source position and evidence scope

Always ask whether a source can actually know the proposition it is being used to support. Position-to-know is claim-specific:

- an official body can authoritatively state its own policy or record, but may be a conflicted evaluator of its own performance;
- an operator or user can directly report local experience, but may not establish population prevalence;
- a journalist can report observed/documented events, but an internal mechanism may still be second-hand;
- a benchmark author can describe the test setup, while independent reproduction is stronger evidence of generalizable performance.

Do not upgrade a source beyond the scope of what it could observe.

## Evidence visibility and private/public routing

When material, tag source visibility as `public`, `user-provided`, `workspace/local-private`, or `confidential/restricted`. Having access for analysis does not authorize publication or sending identifiers/content to public tools. Minimize disclosure and prefer local joins/generalized public queries when private evidence must be combined with public research.

## Provenance clusters

Repeated copies do not create corroboration. Ten articles derived from one press release, wire story, dataset, anonymous post, or interview are one origin cluster for the reused proposition.

Whenever possible, trace derivative reports to the earliest accessible source. Distinguish independent observation from independent publication.

## Weak signals, rumors, leaks, community evidence

Do not exclude evidence solely because it is informal, anonymous, social, niche, or non-authoritative. Weak signals can reveal emerging events, field experience, implementation failures, practitioner behavior, or the existence of a claim before formal confirmation.

But separate:

- **“someone claims X”** — supported by the existence and provenance of the statement;
- **“X is true”** — requires evidence appropriate to X.

For a material weak signal, trace the earliest identifiable source, separate reposts from independent observations, seek corroboration with a different failure/error mechanism, and state what would upgrade or falsify the proposition. Do not collect rumor for entertainment when it cannot affect the user's outcome.

## Confidence language

Use calibrated natural language. Do not invent numerical probabilities unless a defensible model or data supports them. Distinguish supported, contested, inferred, rumor/weak signal, unresolved, inaccessible, and discarded where useful.

## Citation discipline

Citations belong near the exact claim they support. Preserve locators for tables, figures, code, filings, posts, transcripts, or passages when material. Do not make a paragraph-end citation appear to support unrelated clauses. Unread or inaccessible material cannot silently support a strong claim.
